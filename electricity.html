Challenge 1

Challenge 1: Use of Electricity

Two monitors, two desktops, a laptop, two iPads and a phone. The goal was to stop powering devices I wasn't using.

Goals & strategies

By the end of the project I wanted to:

  1. Cut the electricity my devices use on a project day by 50%, which is the target set in the brief.
  2. Build up from two project days a week to four by the final week.
  3. Make the change automatic. Remembering to switch off half a dozen devices every morning is fragile, so the end goal was a setup that does the right thing with almost no effort.

My strategy was to add one layer per week, starting with the easiest change and finishing with automation:

Week 1Switch both monitors off when I leave. Let portable chargers drain to about 10% before recharging instead of topping them up.
Week 2Shut the laptop down completely instead of letting it sleep.
Week 3Shut the Windows desktop and monitors down instead of letting them sleep when idle.
Week 4Turn the shutdown into an automatic routine: everything goes off when I leave for campus and comes back on when I get home.

Two supporting changes run alongside these steps. My Mac's display turns itself off after five minutes of inactivity (Figure E1). I also added a power bar with a single on/off button, so I can cut power to all the chargers plugged into it with one press on the way out, instead of unplugging each one (Figure E4).

Did I reach 50%? An honest estimate

I had no plug-in power meter, so I estimated my savings using typical power draws for each type of device. Standby draws are based on the roughly 0.5 W standby limit for modern electronics (Energy Education, n.d.). These are assumptions, not measurements.

Device (about 9 hours away on a campus day)BeforeAfterSaved / day
2 monitors (~25 W each)on: 0.45 kWhoff: ~0.01 kWh~0.44 kWh
Windows PC (~60 W idle, ~3 W asleep)30 min idle, then asleep: ~0.06 kWhoff: ~0.005 kWh~0.05 kWh
Laptop (~1 W asleep)asleep: ~0.01 kWhoff: 0~0.01 kWh
Total while away~0.52 kWh~0.02 kWh~0.50 kWh

When I include the 5–6 hours I actively use my setup in the evening (around 0.8 kWh either way), a full project day drops from about 1.3 kWh to about 0.8 kWh, a cut of roughly 37%. So I made real progress but fell short of the 50% target. To reach 50%, I would have to use the devices less, not just stop leaving them on.

Across all 11 project days this adds up to about 5 kWh. Ontario's grid emitted about 53 g CO2e per kWh in 2023 (Government of Ontario, n.d.), so that is only about 0.3 kg of CO2e. The small number became one of my biggest takeaways, which I discuss in the conclusion. Standby power still matters at scale, though: it accounts for at least 5% of electricity use in the average Canadian home (Natural Resources Canada, 2014).

What our hydro bills show

Our household's Milton Hydro bill dropped from $231.05 (due October 7) to $181.44 (due November 12), a fall of $49.61, or about 21% (Figures E5 and E6). I believe my habits played a part in that drop: monitors and computers off instead of idling, the laptop shut down instead of sleeping, chargers cut off at the power bar, and less charging overall. They were not the whole story, though. The bill covers everyone in the house, and the change from summer to fall, when air conditioning switches off, also lowers usage. Seeing the bill go down still showed me that changes at my desk reach the household total, even if they are only one part of it.
