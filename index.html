Why I chose these two challenges

As a fourth-year computer engineering student, almost everything I do is plugged in. My desk alone has two monitors, a Windows desktop and a Mac Mini. On top of that I have a laptop, two iPads, a phone and a drawer full of chargers and portable battery packs. Most of my electricity habits were set up once and then forgotten, so I picked use of electricity because it was the area where I was least aware of my own behaviour.

I picked leaving the car at home because it was the change I expected to be hardest. I live in Oakville and study at McMaster in Hamilton, and I also work shifts as a cash supervisor and key holder at Shoppers Drug Mart. Before this project I drove to both because it was the most convenient option. My commute is the longest trip I make on a regular basis, so it was the obvious place to test whether I could change a habit that convenience had locked in.

My typical electricity habits

  • Both monitors stayed on when I left for campus.
  • My computers only went to sleep after a period with no input. They were almost never fully shut down.
  • My laptop sat in sleep mode all day, which drained it and meant charging it several times a day.
  • I topped my portable chargers back up to 100% whenever they dipped, even at 75%.

My typical car habits

  • I drove from Oakville to McMaster on most campus days.
  • I drove myself to and from my shifts at work.
  • Driving was my default because it was faster and felt more reliable than transit.

The project at a glance

I tracked the project over four weeks, from Tuesday, October 6 to Monday, November 2, 2026. The brief asks for the number of project days to grow each week until there are four in the final week. My electricity days did exactly that. My car-free days did not, because midterm season got in the way, and I discuss why on the car page.
