My experience

The two challenges went in opposite directions. Electricity was a steady climb: each week added one layer, and by the end most of it was automatic. The car challenge started strong at three car-free days, then slipped to two as midterms arrived. I followed the plan for one challenge and didn't manage it for the other, and I learned more from the one that didn't go to plan.

Successes

  • I reached four electricity project days in the final week, and the shutdown routine now runs without me having to think about it.
  • I replaced campus drives with the GO bus and used Bike Share for short trips once on campus.
  • I stopped charging things out of reflex. My phone, laptop and power banks get charged when they need it, not whenever they dip.

Failures

  • I didn't reach the 50% electricity cut. Most of the energy I use is active work, not waste, and switching things off doesn't touch that.
  • My car-free days went down instead of up, and I drove to two midterms.
  • My Mac Mini stayed on the whole time because it runs as a home server.

What I learned about my environmental impact

1. The car mattered far more than the plug. Because Ontario's grid is low-carbon, four weeks of careful electricity savings added up to about 0.3 kg of CO2e, while a single bus commute avoided about 16 kg. Before this project I would have guessed the opposite, because unplugging things feels like the classic environmental habit.

2. Reliability decides behaviour more than good intentions do. I didn't drive to my midterms because I didn't care. I drove because transit had let me down once on a high-stakes day. This made the idea of sustainable development, meeting today's needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs (World Commission on Environment and Development, 1987), feel concrete. People pick the sustainable option consistently when it is also the dependable one, so better infrastructure helps as much as individual effort does.

3. Automation beats willpower. The electricity habits that lasted were the ones I removed from my daily decisions: the shutdown routine, the five-minute display timer and the switched power bar. Changes that relied on remembering were the first to slip.

What I will keep doing

KeepThe automatic shutdown routine and the switched power bar.
KeepThe GO bus on regular campus days, and Bike Share for trips around campus.
ChangeOn exam days, take an earlier bus with an hour of buffer instead of driving.
ChangeMeasure next time. A plug-in power meter from day one would replace my estimates with real numbers.
